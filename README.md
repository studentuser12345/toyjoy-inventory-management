# ToyJoy Inventory Management Analysis

## Business Problem

ToyJoy is a seasonal toy retailer operating across stores and online channels. The business needs to maintain enough inventory to satisfy customer demand while avoiding excessive stock that ties up working capital.

The analysis identified an important business challenge: **inventory was not allocated in proportion to the revenue and profit generated by different product categories.**

Puzzles represented approximately 35% of total inventory value but generated only around 30% of revenue and 27% of profit. In contrast, Dolls generated approximately 43% of revenue while representing only 28% of inventory value.

This creates two simultaneous risks:

* **Excess inventory:** Capital is tied up in slower-moving products.
* **Stockout risk:** High-demand products may not have sufficient inventory during peak periods.

The problem becomes more significant because ToyJoy experiences seasonal demand peaks, particularly around March, May, and December. Festival and Summer Holiday periods together account for approximately 41.9% of annual sales volume.

## Business Objective

The objective is to determine:

1. Which categories and products deserve more inventory investment.
2. Which products are carrying excessive inventory.
3. Which products have a higher risk of stockouts.
4. When inventory should be increased ahead of seasonal demand.
5. How ToyJoy can improve inventory efficiency without increasing total inventory investment.

## Business Analysis

The analysis examined category performance, product profitability, inventory allocation, seasonal demand, and inventory risk.

### Category Performance

Dolls are the strongest business driver, leading both revenue and profit. However, their inventory allocation is relatively low compared with the value they generate.

Puzzles have the largest inventory investment but a lower contribution to revenue and profit relative to that investment.

The Inventory Efficiency Index highlights this imbalance:

* Dolls: **1.52 revenue efficiency / 1.61 profit efficiency**
* Puzzles: **0.86 revenue efficiency / 0.77 profit efficiency**
* Action Figures: **0.80 revenue efficiency / 0.68 profit efficiency**
* Board Games: **0.67 revenue efficiency / 0.86 profit efficiency**

An efficiency index above 1 indicates that a category generates a larger share of revenue or profit than its share of inventory value.

### Product Performance

At product level, **Doll House** is the strongest performer, leading both sales volume and profit.

Barbie Doll is another important growth product, while Teddy Bear and Building Blocks have relatively high inventory compared with their sales velocity.

An ABC classification based on profit contribution identifies:

* **A products:** Doll House, Barbie Doll, Teddy Bear
* **B products:** Building Blocks, Jigsaw Puzzle
* **C product:** Action Hero

The A-tier products account for approximately 62% of the profit contribution and therefore require closer inventory monitoring.

### Seasonal Demand

ToyJoy's demand is not consistent throughout the year.

The strongest monthly sales occur in:

* December: **335 units**
* March: **310 units**
* May: **290 units**

Festival and Summer Holiday periods together contribute approximately **1,300 units, or 41.9% of total volume**.

This means inventory planning should not rely on a single year-round reorder strategy.

## Business Solution

The recommended solution is an **inventory rebalancing and demand-driven replenishment strategy.**

### 1. Reallocate Inventory

Reduce excess inventory allocation toward slower-moving categories, particularly Puzzles, and redirect appropriate inventory investment toward high-performing Dolls.

The objective is not to increase total inventory blindly, but to **move inventory toward products that generate greater business value.**

### 2. Prioritize High-Value Products

Doll House and Barbie Doll should receive higher replenishment priority because of their strong sales and profit contribution.

A-tier products should receive tighter monitoring and appropriate safety stock.

Lower-priority products should use leaner replenishment policies.

### 3. Reduce Overstock

Teddy Bear and Building Blocks show signs of overstock risk because their inventory levels are high relative to their sales velocity.

ToyJoy can reduce replenishment frequency or quantities for these products until inventory becomes better aligned with demand.

### 4. Prepare for Seasonal Peaks

Inventory should be positioned **4–6 weeks before major demand peaks**, especially December, March, and May.

This reduces the chance of losing sales because of insufficient stock during high-demand periods.

### 5. Introduce Demand Forecasting

Historical sales and seasonal patterns should be incorporated into a demand forecasting system.

The longer-term objective is to move from reactive replenishment to **predictive inventory planning**.

### 6. Automate Replenishment

Reorder points can be established based on demand thresholds, product priority, and seasonal requirements.

This can eventually create a more self-correcting inventory system where replenishment decisions are triggered by actual demand conditions.

## Expected Business Impact

The proposed strategy targets:

| KPI                |   Current |           Target |
| ------------------ | --------: | ---------------: |
| Revenue            |     ₹2.0M |            ₹2.2M |
| Profit             |     ₹666K |            ₹750K |
| Inventory Turnover |     20.15 |            24.00 |
| Average Inventory  | 164 units |        145 units |
| Stock Availability |   Current | +15% improvement |

The intended business outcome is to **generate more revenue and profit from a leaner and better-allocated inventory base.**

## Final Business Recommendation

ToyJoy should move from a broad inventory allocation strategy toward a **category-, product-, and season-driven inventory strategy**.

The immediate priority is to rebalance inventory away from slower-moving products and toward high-performing Dolls and Doll House. This should be supported by ABC-based inventory prioritization and weekly KPI monitoring.

Over the longer term, ToyJoy should introduce seasonal demand forecasting and automated reorder points.

The overall solution can be summarized as:

**Identify demand → Rebalance inventory → Prioritize profitable products → Forecast seasonal demand → Automate replenishment**

This approach is designed to reduce excess inventory, lower stockout risk, improve inventory turnover, and increase profitability while maintaining customer availability.
